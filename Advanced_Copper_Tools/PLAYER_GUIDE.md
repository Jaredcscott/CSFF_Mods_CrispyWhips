# Advanced Copper Tools: Player Guide

A practical how-to for every item in the mod: how to make it, what it does, and how to use it.

---

## Getting Started

Before anything else, smelt copper and get a hammer. Every recipe in this mod flows from those two things. Suggested build order:

1. **Metal Sheet + Copper Nail** (base materials)
2. **Small Copper Stove** (better campfire with ash/charcoal output)
3. **Wearable Metal Pan** (portable cooking + water purification)
4. **Copper Tea Kettle** (boiling water on a fire or stove)
5. **Large Copper Saw** (faster wood supply)
6. **Copper Armor Set** (helmet, bracers, greaves, torso armor), then the alloy suits
7. **Metal Lantern + Oil Chain** (portable night light)
8. **Wheelbarrow** (haul more weight)
9. **Copper Cauldron** (batch cooking)
10. **Copper Bathtub** (deep cleansing + morale)
11. **Tea Blending Station** (herb processing hub)
12. **Copper Chest** (long-term sealed storage)

## Metal Sheet & Copper Nail

The building blocks of everything else in the mod.

**To craft:** Heat the metal in a forge or fire until it glows, then use a hammer on it.

| Item | Inputs | Build Time |
|------|--------|:----------:|
| **Metal Sheet** | 1 heated copper-grade metal bar + hammer (not consumed) | 2 ticks |
| **Bronze Sheet** | 1 heated tin or bronze metal bar (Ghost Bronze, Tin, Tin Bronze or White Bronze) + hammer (not consumed) | 2 ticks |
| **Copper Nail** | 1 heated copper, tin or bronze nugget + hammer (not consumed); the nail is the nugget's metal | 2 ticks |
| **Cast Copper Nails** | 1 heated copper, tin or bronze bar + 1 mud brick + 1 clay as the mould; 6 nails of the bar's metal | 3 ticks |
| **Iron Sheet** | 6 iron nuggets + hammer (not consumed); a Metal Sheet of iron | 4 ticks |
| **Forge Iron Nails** | 4 iron nuggets + hammer (not consumed); 4 Metal Nails of iron | 4 ticks |

**One sheet card, one nail card:** every sheet is the one Metal Sheet card and every nail the one Metal Nail card, iron included: an Iron Sheet is a Metal Sheet of iron and an Iron Nail a Metal Nail of iron, each with its own name and picture. Iron recipes (iron armor, the Iron Shield, the Iron Large Saw) take only Iron Sheets. The recipes whose product takes the sheet's metal but has no iron form (Small Copper Stove, Large Saw, Copper Cauldron, Copper Oil Flask, Forged Pan Blank, Lantern Oilwell, Metal Lantern, Wheelbarrow Bucket) refuse Iron Sheets. An Iron Sheet or Iron Nail from an older save turns into the new card by itself within a quarter of an hour, or at once with its **Reforge** button.

**Sheet metal types:** all three sheet recipes make the same Metal Sheet card, named for the metal it was hammered from (Copper Sheet, Ghost Bronze Sheet and so on). Each alloy armor suit needs sheets of its own metal, so it matters which one you pick up. Sheets of different metals pile together; use the pile's expand toggle to spread them out and drag the one you want. Nails take the metal of their nugget (Tin Nail, White Bronze Nail and so on), but a nail of any metal works the same in every recipe. Sheets, pan heads, pans, stove tops, cauldrons, nails, Large Saws and Small Stoves each show their own metal's picture (art by Chiwei), except that a pan head heated above 150 Temperature shows one glowing picture for every metal; the Stove Top Mold and the Oil Flask still show one picture for every metal.

**Smelting back:** Most metal items melt back down to nuggets in a hot forge; see Smelting Recovery below.

## Small Copper Stove

A portable fireplace that holds cooking like a campfire. Burns longer than a campfire and produces charcoal and ash. It weighs 600, and placing it or picking it up takes about 3 minutes.

**Recipe (two stages):** first cast the stove top with a Small Molten Crucible (not consumed), 2 mud bricks and 2 clay; then assemble it from 4 metal sheets of one metal + 4 copper nails + a forge hammer, mallet, stone, antler or shovel (not consumed) + 1 Tin Solder. The stove is the metal of its sheets (Small Tin Stove, Small White Bronze Stove and so on) and keeps it when placed, lit, put out and picked up. A Stove Top or Stove Top Mold from an older save still works as an item: Break the mold with a hammer, then use Cut Up on the Stove Top to get 2 metal sheets of its metal.

**Unlock:** once you hold a Metal Sheet, the stove can be researched (64 ticks). The Cast Stove Top Mold blueprint is gone: the stove casts its own top.

**How to use:**
1. Place the stove anywhere (outdoors or in a sheltered location).
2. Add firewood, twigs, or charcoal as fuel. Fuel capacity: 300 units (oak +96, charcoal +8, twigs +24).
3. Drag a fire source (tinder, torch, another fire) onto the stove to light it.
4. Place food items or liquid containers on it. It holds 600 weight, the same as a Campfire: one cooking pot or two clay bowls. A clay or copper cauldron is too heavy for it.
5. The stove generates charcoal and ash during burning. Collect them for other uses.

**Around the fire:** a lit stove works like a campfire for you: it dries you out, keeps bugs away and lights the camp. Use **Warm Up** or **Wait by Fire** on it to warm yourself. Like any open fire it also makes you easier to spot, so hunters may want it out.

**Comfort:** a placed stove, lit or not, adds Sanctuary to where it stands: +75 in copper or Tin Bronze, +80 in Ghost Bronze, +65 in Tin, +90 in White Bronze.

**Burn time:** a full lit stove burns about 75 hours in copper or White Bronze, 83 in Ghost Bronze, 60 in Tin and 94 in Tin Bronze. These per-metal numbers may change in a later version.

**Pick up:** Extinguish first, then use the Pick Up action. You can carry it to a new location and place it again; the fuel left in it goes with it.

**Used as a component:** The Small Copper Stove is required in the Copper Bathtub blueprint.

## Wearable Metal Pan

A multi-metal frying pan that you can wear, cook in, and use to purify water.

**Recipe:**
1. Hammer 1 Metal Sheet into a **Shaped Metal Pan Head** with a forge hammer, mallet, stone, antler or shovel (not consumed).
2. Combine the pan head + 3 twine + 1 wood + the same kind of hammering tool (not consumed) + 1 Tin Solder → **Wearable Metal Pan**.

**Unlock:** once you hold a Metal Sheet, the Wearable Metal Pan can be researched (32 ticks) and the Forged Pan Blank blueprint is unlocked at the same time, with no research of its own.

**Cooking:** Place the pan on any lit fire source. Put food items in the pan to cook them directly.

**Water purification:** Fill the pan with river or dirty water, then place it on a lit fire. Heat propagates through the pan to the held liquid, and the vanilla boil-to-safe-water transform runs automatically.

**Wearing it:** Equip in a quiver-style slot for hands-free transport: the pan travels with you without taking up inventory space. It weighs 300 in your pack and nothing while worn (like the vanilla waterskin), holds 1600, and up to 1600 weight of what it holds does not count toward your load, as with the vanilla Cooking Pot.

**Available metals:** Copper, ghost bronze, tin, tin bronze, and white bronze. One blueprint covers them all: the pan takes the metal of the sheet you hammer, and piles of pans or pan heads of different metals have the expand toggle so you can pick one out.

**To recover:** Dismantle the wearable pan to get back the shaped pan head and the parts that went into it.

**Panning for tin:** At any place with a river, the wearable pan offers **Pan for Stream Tin** (the unassembled pan head does not). Each try takes an hour and has a 15% chance of one Tin Ore, which smelts like mined ore. Each try costs the wearable pan 1 of its 100 uses. It is slow, but it gets Tin Solder going before you reach the Tin Vein Cave.

## Large Copper Saw

A two-handed saw that fells large trees significantly faster than any vanilla axe.

**Recipe:** 3 metal sheets of one metal + 2 wood + 2 copper nails + hammer (not consumed) + 1 Tin Solder. The saw is the metal of its sheets.

**How to use:** Drag the saw onto a tree (just like any cutting tool). On large trees (pine, oak, birch, willow) the saw adds extra damage on top of the vanilla −25, and how much, and how fast it wears, depends on its metal:

| Saw | Large pine or willow | Large birch | Large oak | Lasts about |
|-----|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Copper | 1 pull | 2 pulls | 2 pulls | 100 pulls |
| Ghost Bronze | 1 pull | 2 pulls | 2 pulls | 166 pulls |
| Tin | 2 pulls | 2 pulls | 3 pulls | 66 pulls |
| Tin Bronze | 1 pull | 2 pulls | 2 pulls | 200 pulls |
| White Bronze | 1 pull | 1 pull | 2 pulls | 80 pulls |

These per-metal numbers may change in a later version.

**Small trees:** Works normally through the standard `tag_Axe` tree-cutting interaction; no extra bonus on small trees.

**Tags:** `tag_Axe` and `tag_AdvancedAxe`, so it works as an advanced axe for all tree interactions.

### Iron Large Saw

**Recipe:** 2 Iron Sheets + 2 wood + 4 nails of any metal + hammer (not consumed). It unlocks once you hold an Iron Sheet and needs no Tin Solder.

It adds **−50 Progress** instead of −25, so −75 per swing: a large pine, willow or birch falls in one swing and a large oak in two. It lasts 150 uses against the copper saw's 100.

## Copper Armor Set

Four copper armor pieces can be equipped for body-zone protection. They are crafted in **Construction → Metal Tools**; the Helmet and Armor unlock once you have a Metal Sheet, the Bracers and Greaves once you have a Heated Metal Bar. They take copper only: a tin, bronze or iron sheet or bar does not fit.

| Piece | Protects | Requirements |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| **Copper Helmet** | Head +30 | 2 metal sheets + 4 copper nails + 1 small leather + 2 sinew + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |
| **Copper Bracers** | Arms +15 each | 2 heated metal bars + 4 copper nails + 1 small leather + 2 sinew + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |
| **Copper Greaves** | Legs +15 each | 2 heated metal bars + 4 copper nails + 1 small leather + 2 sinew + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |
| **Copper Armor** | Torso +30 | 4 metal sheets + 4 copper nails + 1 medium leather + 4 sinew + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |

The hammer is used as a tool and is not consumed. Each piece melts back down to nuggets in a forge (see Smelting Recovery). At full durability, the complete copper set fills the Armor stat when worn over the usual leather gloves, shoes, tunic, and trousers.

### Shields

Three wooden shields faced with metal, all in **Construction → Metal Tools**. Equip one to hold it in hand, like the wooden Shield. It draws blows toward your shield arm. The wooden Shield gives 75 armor, or 125 against spiritual attacks, and lasts 5000. Dismantle any of them for sheets of their own metal, a plank, nails and a medium leather you can use. The numbers for the Iron and Ghost Bronze Shields are placeholders that may change.

| Shield | Recipe (hammer is not consumed) | Arm armor (vs spiritual) | Durability | Melts to |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Copper Shield | 3 copper sheets + 1 plank + 4 copper nails + 1 medium leather + 1 Tin Solder, once you have a Metal Sheet | 90 (140) | 8000 | 22 copper nuggets |
| Iron Shield | 3 iron sheets + 1 plank + 4 nails of any metal + 1 medium leather, once you have an Iron Sheet | 110 (160) | 10000 | 30 iron nuggets |
| Ghost Bronze Shield | 3 Ghost Bronze sheets + 1 plank + 4 copper nails + 1 medium leather + 1 Tin Solder, once you have a Ghost Bronze sheet | 95 (175) | 9000 | 25 Ghost Bronze nuggets |

The Iron Shield blocks the most against any blow. The Ghost Bronze Shield blocks a little less in general but is the best ward against spiritual attacks, which is how most animals strike. With Community Mod Chest, the Iron and Ghost Bronze Shields are not yet taught by the Armorer course and use normal research.

### Alloy Armor Sets

Tin, Ghost Bronze, Tin Bronze and White Bronze each make their own four-piece suit, from sheets of that metal only. Hammer a heated bar of the metal on the **Bronze Sheet** blueprint; the suit's recipes appear once you hold one of its sheets. Every piece needs the same things as its copper counterpart (2 sheets, or 4 for the Armor), except that Tin pieces use 2 copper nails and no Tin Solder.

| Suit (Metal Type) | Head / Torso | Arms / Legs (each) | Durability | Special |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Copper (100) | 30 | 15 | 100 | the cheap first suit |
| Tin (120) | 32.5 | 16 | 90 | the lightest metal suit; no Tin Solder and 2 copper nails a piece |
| Ghost Bronze (110) | 35 | 16.5 | 120 | +12.5 head and torso, +7.5 each arm and leg against spiritual attacks; each piece makes Fear fall 0.25 faster per tick |
| Tin Bronze (130) | 37.5 | 17.5 | 120 | the toughest bronze |
| White Bronze (140) | 42.5 | 19 | 100 | +3 Courage per piece |
| Iron (200) | 45 | 20 | 140 | the best raw protection |

**Which one?** Ghost Bronze is for animal fights: wolves, bears, foxes, badgers and beavers attack with purely spiritual blows, and against those a Ghost Bronze piece blocks more than iron. White Bronze is the best armor short of iron and steadies your nerve. Tin is the step up when you have tin but no crucible. Tin Bronze (the old "Bronze" set) lasts longest. Dismantle a piece for sheets of its own metal, or melt it for nuggets of that metal.

## Wheelbarrow

A wearable cargo container that reduces the effective weight of everything you carry in it.

**Recipe (4 sub-assemblies):**

| Component | Recipe |
|-----------|--------|
| **Wheelbarrow Bucket** | 8 metal sheets + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |
| **Wheelbarrow Handles** | 3 planks + 2 small leather + 2 long sticks + 4 copper nails + sharp knife + hammer |
| **Wheel Rim** | 1 heated metal bar + hammer |
| **Wheel Hub** | Forged: 3 copper nuggets + hammer, or Cast: 2 mud bricks + 2 clay with a Small Molten Crucible (not consumed) |
| **Wheel Assembly** | 1 rim + 1 hub + 1 wood + hammer + 1 Tin Solder |
| **Wheelbarrow** | Bucket + handles + wheel assembly + 1 Tin Solder |

**How to use:** Equip the Wheelbarrow. Items you load into it weigh less than they would in your hands. Permanent: does not degrade from normal use.

**Dismantle:** Returns bucket, handles, and wheel assembly.

**Note:** The Wheelbarrow Bucket is also a required component in the Copper Bathtub blueprint.

## Copper Bathtub

A 3-state relaxation and cleansing structure. One of the best morale investments in the mod.

**Recipe:** 1 Wheelbarrow Bucket + 1 Small Copper Stove + 4 planks + 8 mud bricks + 1 large cloth + 4 long sticks + 3 small leather + hammer + 1 Tin Solder.

### The Three States

**Empty Bathtub**
- Place it anywhere.
- Load fuel (firewood, charcoal) into the stove underneath before filling.
- Fill from a water container or directly from a river (drag a filled container onto the tub).

**Full (Cold)**
- Take a **cold bath**: cleans one step below the river (Filth -400), clears rashes and wolfsbane, modest morale boost. You come out wet.
- To heat: Add fuel, then drag a fire source (tinder/torch) to light the stove.

**Full (Warm)** (Heat stat: 0–96, drains ~−0.25/daytime point ≈ 1 in-game day of warmth)
- Take a **warm bath**: cleans as well as the river (Filth -500) and clears rashes and wolfsbane, with a major morale boost, spiritual benefit and body warmth. Keep the heat above half for a **hot bath**: the same wash with a bigger morale boost and less Stress. You come out wet.
- Booked as one of the best available morale/hygiene actions in the game.

### Other Actions
- **Wash with Soap** - drag vanilla Soap onto the warm tub for a clean as good as soap anywhere else (Filth -400, rashes and wolfsbane cleared) and a mood boost, without lighting the fire
- **Drying off** - every bath leaves you wet; use Wait by Fire at a lit Small Copper Stove or Copper Brazier to dry
- **Add Firewood / Add Fuel**: top up the fuel during heating
- **Empty**: drain the water when done
- **Pick Up**: carry the tub to a new location (works whether it is empty, full or warm; a filled tub is picked up as a filled tub)
- **Dismantle**: recover materials including the wheelbarrow bucket and stove

## Metal Lantern (4-Variant Portable Light)

A portable oil-burning light source that exists in four states: item/placed × lit/unlit. Fuel carries across every transform.

### The Four States

| State | Card Type | Carryable | Light output |
|-------|-----------|:---------:|:------------:|
| **Metal Lantern** (item, unlit) | Item | Yes | None: refuel here |
| **Metal Lantern** (item, lit) | Item | Yes | Carried light |
| **Placed Lantern** (unlit) | Placed | No | None: refuel here |
| **Placed Lantern** (lit) | Placed | No | Area light |

**Recipe:** 1 metal sheet + 1 Lantern Oilwell + 1 copper wire + 1 parchment + 1 fat + a hammer (the parchment and fat make the oiled shade; no glass). The lantern takes the sheet's metal.

### Fueling the Lantern

Pour any lamp oil in a clay bowl (ACT's **Oil**, or with Herbs and Fungi installed its hemp seed, linseed, peanut, truffle and herbal oils) directly onto the unlit lantern and you get the bowl back, or drag a **Copper Oil Flask** onto it to pour in one bowl's worth per drag. Each bowl of oil is worth 48 fuel and the lantern holds 96, so two bowls fill it and it burns about 48 hours.

The **Lantern Oilwell** can be fuelled and lit on its own too: two bowls fill it for 24 hours of a smaller light. These numbers are tunable placeholders.

### Lighting

Drag any fire source (tinder, torch, campfire) onto an unlit lantern with ≥10% fuel. The lantern ignites.

### When Fuel Runs Out

Lit variants automatically extinguish back to their unlit counterpart. No manual action needed: the lantern just goes dark.

### Placing and Picking Up

- Drag the held lantern item to place it (unlit) as a fixed light source.
- Pick up a placed (unlit) lantern to carry it. If lit, extinguish first.

## Oil Chain

Rendered animal fat produces **Oil**, a lamp fuel that also provides nutrition in a pinch.

| Blueprint | Recipe | Where |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Rendered Oil** | 2 animal fat + 1 clay bowl | Support tab |
| **Render Fish Oil** | 2 fatty raw fish meat + 1 clay bowl | Support tab |
| **Render Hemp Seed Oil** | 1 hemp seed oil → 1 oil | Support tab (requires H&F installed) |
| **Lantern Oilwell** | 1 metal sheet + 1 twine + hammer + 1 Tin Solder | Metal Tools tab |
| **Copper Oil Flask** | 2 metal sheets + 1 medium leather + hammer + 1 Tin Solder | Metal Tools tab |

**Copper Oil Flask:** Holds 4 bowls of oil (192 fuel). Drag the flask onto an unlit lantern, oilwell or brazier to pour in one bowl's worth (48). A full flask refuels the lantern from empty to full twice over.

**Render Hemp Seed Oil:** Requires the Herbs and Fungi mod to be installed (the hemp seed oil ingredient comes from H&F's oil press chain).

**Any lamp oil works:** the lantern, the Copper Brazier and the oil flask take any lamp oil served in a clay bowl, not only rendered Oil, so you can burn Herbs and Fungi oils directly without converting them first.

## Bedwarming Pan

A lidded copper pan on a long handle that keeps you warm through one night.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 1 copper sheet + 1 long stick + 2 copper nails. The recipe appears once you hold a Metal Sheet.
- **Fill it:** drag the pan onto a burning campfire (or any fire) and choose Fill with Embers. You get a Hot Bedwarming Pan.
- **Use it:** put the hot pan into your bed (a log bed or an improvised bed; a bedroll takes anything) and sleep. You feel 3 degrees warmer while asleep, a little more than a large cloth gives.
- **By morning it is cold.** The embers last about ten hours, awake or asleep, and then it is an empty pan again. Fill it again the next evening.

The warmth and the ten hours are tunable placeholders.

## Copper-Buckled Tool Belt

A leather belt with a copper buckle that carries your hammer, knives and chisel lighter than your pack.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 1 copper sheet + 1 medium leather + 4 copper nails, with a hammer. The recipe appears once you hold a Metal Sheet.
- **Wear it** on its own Tool Belt slot. It does not take the place of a knife sheath or a spear sheath, so you can wear all three.
- **Put tools in it:** hammers, knives and the chisel go in; saws and everything else do not. What is inside weighs 200 less, and the worn belt itself weighs 250 less.
- **Tools in it still count as on you** for actions that look for one in hand.
- **Blueprints:** a blueprint's slot does not take tools from your belt. Drag the hammer out of the belt onto the blueprint as usual.

The weights are tunable placeholders.

## Cautery Iron

A copper rod with a flat oval head on a wooden handle. It closes an open cut with heat.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 1 copper bar + 1 long stick. The recipe appears once you hold a heated metal ingot.
- **Heat it:** drag the iron onto a burning fire. You get a Hot Cautery Iron that stays hot for about an hour.
- **Cauterize:** drag the hot iron onto an open laceration (an arm, a leg or a minor laceration) and choose Cauterize. The cut closes at once, no needle and thread, and you take one Fire Burn wound.
- **The price:** the burn, a sharp pain that fades over the following hours, and some stress. Stitching with a needle stays painless, so cautery is for when you have no thread or no time.
- Bites, claw marks, punctures and other wounds that have no stitched form cannot be cauterized.

The pain, the stress and the hour of heat are tunable placeholders.

## Copper Spigot

A short forged copper spout. Set into a sap tap or a resin tap, it makes the tap run about three days instead of one.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 1 copper sheet + 1 tin solder. The recipe appears once you hold a Metal Sheet.
- **Fit it:** drag the spigot onto a placed Sap Tap or Resin Tap and choose Fit Spigot. The tap keeps its bowl and the sap in it.
- **Remove it:** choose Remove Spigot on the fitted tap to get the plain tap, its bowl and the spigot back.
- A fitted tap still needs a Refresh with a knife when its flow bar is empty, only about a third as often.

The one-third rate is a tunable placeholder.

## Copper Sap Bucket

A small copper bucket that hangs in a tapped tree.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 2 metal sheets + 1 tin solder, with a hammer. The recipe appears once you hold a Metal Sheet.
- **Sap:** put it in a Sap Tap. It catches the sap and holds 900, a whole flow of a tap with a spigot.
- **Resin:** put it in a Resin Tap (Spigot). It gathers one lump of resin every 18 hours, up to four. Choose Scrape Out Resin on the bucket to take a lump out.

## Grappling Hook

Three copper prongs on a coil of rope. Carry it and every climb is more likely to succeed.

- **Craft it** in Construction → Metal Tools: 3 copper nuggets + 1 rope, with a hammer. The recipe appears once you hold a metal nugget.
- **Use it:** just carry it. It raises your Climbing Aid by 72 while it is anywhere on you, including inside a bag or basket you carry.
- It never wears out, and it works on every climb because it raises the stat the game already uses for climbing.

The +72 is a tunable placeholder.

## Copper Brazier

A copper fire-bowl on a stick tripod that burns lamp oil. Place the kit, pour in any lamp oil (24 per clay bowl, bowl returned) and light it with a fire source. While lit it lights a wide area and works like an open fire: it dries you out, keeps bugs and mosquitoes away, and makes you easier to spot. Use **Warm Up** or **Wait by Fire** on it to warm yourself. Extinguish it before refueling or packing it up; the oil left in it goes back into the kit.

## Copper Tea Kettle

A liquid container that heats water on a lit fire or stove.

**Recipe:** 2 metal sheets + hammer (not consumed) + 1 Tin Solder. The water in it weighs nothing.

**How to use:**
1. Fill the kettle with water (drag a water source or water container onto it).
2. Place the filled kettle on a lit fire or stove. Like a vanilla pot, it fits only a fire with room for its weight: the kettle weighs 180 and its water weighs nothing (content weight reduction 2400, its full capacity), so even a full kettle is 180 and fits a Campfire or the Small Copper Stove (capacity 600 each) as well as a Fireplace, Fire Pit or Oven.
3. The kettle's temperature rises via heat-transfer; the liquid runs its own boil-to-safe-water transform once hot enough.
4. Remove from fire when boiled. Kettle cools down naturally once off the heat source.

**Capacity:** 200-unit Temperature stat; dissipates naturally when removed from the heat source.

## Copper Cauldron

A large batch cooking vessel with a weight-limited ingredient basket for cooking many items at once.

**Recipe:** 5 metal sheets + 4 copper nails + hammer (not consumed) + 1 Tin Solder.

**How to use:**
1. Place the cauldron into a lit Fireplace or Fire Pit. It weighs 600 (the clay cauldron weighs 1200) and is 25% larger than the clay cauldron. Fires take it by weight against their own capacity, and the Oven refuses it.
2. Load `tag_Cookable` or `tag_Boilable` ingredients into the basket (8000 weight capacity; how many fit depends on their weight).
3. The fire heats the cauldron; recipes run on the contents.
4. Remove cooked items and repeat.

**Liquid brewing:** The cauldron holds 6000 ml of liquid. Fill it with water and add ingredients for batch-brewed soups, stews, or teas.

**Tip:** The Copper Cauldron is far more efficient than individual clay bowls for batch cooking: load it up and let it run while you do other tasks.

## Tea Blending Station (3-Variant)

A dedicated herb-processing workbench with six slots, a built-in water reservoir, and a Grind All button.

### The Three Variants

| State | Type | Pickable | Notes |
|-------|------|:--------:|-------|
| **Tea Station Kit** | Item | Yes | Carry and place when ready |
| **Tea Station (unlit)** | Placed | Yes | Drying and grinding; picking it up sets the slot contents down beside you |
| **Tea Station (lit)** | Placed | No | Full function; extinguish before picking up |

**Recipe:** 2 planks + 4 twine + 1 Copper Tea Kettle + 1 Small Copper Stove + 1 rotary quern + 15 heavy stones.

### Using the Six Herb Slots

Place fresh herbs, mushrooms, or dry-able items directly into the six slots:
- Items dry passively over time, even unlit.
- Lighting the stove speeds up drying and unlocks heating/cooking/water-boiling inside the slots.
- Suitable items: anything tagged `tag_Dryable` or `tag_DryableFastSpoilable`.

### Grind All

The "Grind All" action button reads each slot item's own Grind interaction and grinds everything in one step (30 minutes). Works on any dried or millable item, with no need to drag grinding tools individually. Each powder keeps the freshness and quality a hand grind would give it, so an old herb makes old powder.

### Water Reservoir (built-in, 8 bowls of water)

1. Drag clay bowls of water onto the station to fill the reservoir.
2. **Draw Cold Water:** drag an empty bowl onto the station → fills it from the reservoir.
3. **Draw Boiled Water (Hot):** when the station is lit and the reservoir is at 50%+ heat, drag an empty bowl → fills it with boiled water and consumes one charge.

The lit station heats its own reservoir: cold water in a lit station warms to usable temperature in about 30 in-game minutes.

### Lighting and Extinguishing

- **Light:** Drag a fire source (tinder/torch) onto the unlit station (requires ≥10% fuel).
- **Extinguish:** DismantleAction button.
- **Fuel:** Standard firewood/charcoal; same capacity as the Small Copper Stove.

### Picking Up

The station must be **unlit** before it can be picked up: Pick Up only appears on the unlit station, so extinguish it first. The Kit has no slots, so anything still in the six slots is set down beside you instead of being carried.

## Copper Chest

A sealed, insulated storage chest that keeps food fresh far longer than open storage.

**Recipe:** 4 metal sheets + 4 planks + 6 copper nails + hammer + 1 Tin Solder.

**Spoilage reduction:** Items stored inside spoil at **20% of their normal rate**, five times slower than leaving them out.

**Animals:** Vanilla wildlife cannot raid the Copper Chest. Unlike the Wooden Pantry, it lacks the tag that animals can detect and target. If Harsh Wilds is installed, its autumn black bear can still destroy meat or fish stored in a placed Copper Chest.

**Use case:** Long-term preservation of dried herbs, cured meats, or rare ingredients you want to stockpile across seasons.

## Copper Watering Can

- **Fill:** drag the can onto a river, pond or other shallow water source. It holds 1500 ml.
- **Water a garden plot:** drag the filled can onto a garden plot. Each watering takes 300 ml from the can, so a full can waters five times.
- **Drink or empty:** Drink in a pinch, or Empty Out onto the ground.

## Salt-Cured Meat and Salt-Cured Fish

- **Craft:** hold some Salt and both recipes appear in Survival, Support. Meat takes 1 raw meat + 2 Salt; fish takes 1 raw lean fish + 2 Salt.
- **Keeps:** both spoil far slower than the dried version (the fish lasts over twice as long as vanilla dried lean fish).
- **Eat:** the salt costs a little Hydration. The fish is lighter than the meat.
- **Partners:** an allied Partner eats both, and rendered Oil, when hungry, since all three count as human food (not yet confirmed in play); it leaves teas and the Watering Can alone.

## Mother Lodes

The Copper Vein Cave and the Iron Vein Cave each hide one collapsed seam, a **Copper Lode** or an **Iron Lode**. Drag a pickaxe (or a shovel, axe, antler or knife) onto it to **Clear the Seam**: each strike takes two hours, removes 1 of its 18 Rubble and wears the tool by 2. After the eighteenth strike the seam is a mother lode: **Mine** it with a pickaxe or **Chip Away** with an axe, hammer, shovel or antler, a little slower than the ordinary veins (2 hours 30 minutes a strike, like the game's own copper mine, with its Stamina cost), and it never runs out. Caves you built before this version have no lode; a new run or a cave first visited after this version does.

## The Mines Are Dark

All five ACT caves are dark. Without a light you cannot Mine, Chip Away, Clear the Seam or Dig Through: the game says "It's too dark for this action...". Carry a lit Metal Lantern or a torch, or set down a lit lantern, Copper Brazier or Small Copper Stove in the cave, and you can work. You can always leave a cave in the dark: travel never needs light.

The Tin Vein Cave (Metal Mines) has a **Daylight Shaft**, a small hole in the roof. From 8:00 to 16:00 it lets in just enough light to see and dig by; at night that cave is dark too. Caught there with no light? Wait for morning, then dig out.

## Tips

- **Build the stove early.** Charcoal output from the copper stove makes heating copper much faster, since it generates its own fuel.
- **Equip the pan and wheelbarrow together.** You cook on the go and carry more weight at the same time.
- **The tea station replaces multiple items.** It dries herbs, grinds them, heats water, and brews tea all in one place, and is worth the resource cost.
- **Oil flasks extend lantern life.** Carry a full flask and you'll never be caught in the dark: its 4 bowls refill an empty lantern twice.
- **Bathtub water lasts about a day before it cools.** Heat it just before your rest cycle for maximum morale benefit.
