# Community Mod Chest

**Version:** 1.45.0

**Language:** English, Simplified Chinese

A collection of community-suggested content additions for Card Survival: Fantasy Forest.

## Contents

- **Apparel** — cloth scarf, cloth mask, hand wraps, foot wraps, leather sandals, leather apron, cloth coat, long johns, straw hat, chaperon, quilted cap
- **Weapons & armor** — stone mace (blunt weapon, scales with and trains the Melee Fighting skill), fire-hardened spear (full vanilla spear moveset plus a signature Charred-Tip Strike attack dealing bonus Char damage), wooden shield, bone-tier armor (quilted vest, bone lamellar) with working combat damage reduction
- **Character-creation traits** — 42 perks across two categories (plus six hidden "Graduate" perks granted only at runtime by the Village Academy — see below — which stay out of character creation and so aren't counted here, and the retired **Higher Education** trait, kept only so old saves that picked it stay valid):
  - *Positive*: Angler, Born Mark, Claws, Abundant Growth, Exiled Artist, Green Thumb, Homestead (100 Suns — start with a Cabin Kit and two Rain Cistern Kits, each carryable and placeable once wherever you choose, plus a stockpile of building materials: 30 Planks, 30 Mud Bricks, 30 Heavy Stones, 50 Stones, 10 Tree Logs, 50 Copper Nails, 10 Rope), Mineral Rich, Natural Trader, Past Life Memories, Peaceful Farmer, Prospector, Spiritually Harmonious, Strong Kidneys, Swimmer, Trapper, Troglodyte, Trollblood, Wide Hands; Village Pathfinder (gates the village area); Village Inn (a head start with the Inn Keeper — +5 starting friendship, and he waives the formal introductions, so the village timeline starts open); Village Founder (3 Stars — skip straight to a fully-built village: both cottages, the Village Hall, and the Apothecary's Cabin already standing, residents already moved in, and all four villagers' first errands already settled)
  - *Drawback*: Aged, Agoraphobia, Bad Kidneys, Black Thumb, Bleeder, Deadly Disease, Drunkard, Fugitive, Insomniac, Lactose Intolerant, Leper, Lost Tourist, Lunacy, Nyctophobia, Seasonal Allergies, Sensitive Skin, Sinker, Spiritually Troubled, Weak Stomach, Weakened Growth
- **Pottery & sculpting** — painted bowls, cups, plates, and vases; clay figurines (bear, castle, dragon); clay beads, pigment
- **Decorations & furnishings** — woven wall hanging, tricolor wall hanging, stone tiles (lay-able as a **Stone Tile Floor** — see below)
- **Comfort & rest** — heather wreath, wind chimes, bedroll (placed), straw mat (placed), incense burner, **Comfortable Bed** — a Down Mattress (Feathers ×100, Fibers ×50, Large Cloth ×4, Twine ×20) and a Copper Bed Frame (Copper Sheet ×3, Wood ×6, Plank ×2 — **requires Advanced Copper Tools**) are crafted as separate components, then assembled into a placed CT2 bed with its own Nap/Sleep actions (stronger rest recovery than the Bedroll) and a passive perceived-temperature bonus; a Take Apart action splits it back into both components to move camp
- **Lighting** — ceramic lamp
- **Fishing** — fishing net, iron fishing rod (crafted from iron fishing rod fittings)
- **Crafting materials** — herb paste (ground from Wild Garlic + Old Growth Bark)
- **Hunting Stand** — a reward blueprint unlocked partway through the Miller's grain errand chain: build a kit (3 Planks, 4 Long Sticks, 2 Rope), set it up at a location, and wild-animal encounters there have a chance to be averted while you're stationed there
- **Meaningful NPC trading (`TradingValues.json`)** — a price table covering 653 vanilla items and liquids that ship with `TradingValue: 0` (planks, rope, berries, meat, hides, pottery, tools, carcasses, brews, …), so NPC trades no longer hand them out for free. Prices are calibrated to vanilla's own scale (salt 5, herb powders ~17, dried meat 40, metal nugget 250, cloth clothing 1500). Truly abundant materials (stones, leaves, needles, twigs, grass, snow, water) and waste (ash, manure, rotten things) deliberately stay at 0. Requires framework 2.18.0+; on older frameworks the table is inert and trading stays vanilla
- **Village area** — twelve explorable locations east of the River Clearing, accessed by building the **River Bridge** environment improvement at the River Clearing (requires Advanced Copper Tools), wired via `WorldMap/MapNodes.json`. The village hub sits in the middle, with a trail running north and another running south:
  - **Village Path** — staging node, unlocked by the Village Pathfinder trait (perk-gated via `WorldMap/MapNodes.json` ConnectionGates)
  - **Hunter's Crossing** — pine hunting terrain directly between Village Path and Pine Trail (a shortcut connecting the two); badgers den here
  - **Village Farm** — a fertile meadow; forage **Wild Garlic** (15% on any Forage action), discover the **Garden Trellis** blueprint, and harvest a seasonal crop field (Flax in spring, Turnroot in summer, Rye in autumn, none in winter) — each field gives a limited number of harvests before it's spent for the season
  - **Village** — a small settlement; an enterable **Village Inn** and **Village Academy** stand here from day one, and the **Market Stall** can also be set up here. The northern trail continues from here.
  - **Mossy Clearing → Pine Trail → High Grove** — the northern trail beyond the Village; three additional wooded stops for players who want to range further before turning back
  - **Deer Meadow → Badger Warren** — new hunting terrain extending north past the Village; deer frequent Deer Meadow, badgers den at Badger Warren. Badger Warren also opens a one-way passage west into the vanilla **Greenfalls** — a shortcut out of the village area, not a return route
  - **Clay Flats** — the southern trail's first stop past Village Path, a marshy riverside flat. Home to the **Clay Mound**: dig it with a shovel for the best yield (2-3 Clay per dig), or work it loose with any other digging/chopping tool for a smaller yield. The mound has limited durability and is eventually spent.
  - **Marsh Hollow** — a boggy alder hollow between Clay Flats and the Foraging Forest
  - **Foraging Forest** — moved further out along the southern trail (past Clay Flats and Marsh Hollow) and re-themed as a pine deer-wood; forage **Old Growth Bark** (10%) and **Wild Garlic** (8%); if H&F is installed, rare mushrooms also drop here. Deer are known to frequent these woods
- **Wild Garlic** — a perishable food herb forageable at the Village Farm and Foraging Forest; modest satiation/hydration
- **Old Growth Bark** — a crafting material forageable only at the Foraging Forest; the other ingredient (with Wild Garlic) in Herb Paste
- **Clay Mound** — a natural clay deposit at Clay Flats; a `CardType 2` landmark with two dig interactions (Dig Clay with a shovel, or Scoop Clay with any other digging/chopping tool) that produce raw Clay before the mound is exhausted
- **Garden Trellis** — a CT2 placed structure (StickLong ×6, Twine ×8); provides a Comfort +12 passive; researched in the Furniture crafting tab after visiting the Village Farm; weathers away after 6 in-game days
- **Stone Tile Floor** — a CT10 environment improvement buildable inside cabins and mud huts (main rooms, expansions, and the free-build construction rooms); consumes Stone Tiles ×12 + Clay ×4 and takes 2 hours to lay. Grants Comfort +12 and Insulation (+1 perceived temperature) in that room. Shows up in the room's improvements panel once you have at least one Stone Tile
- **Market Stall** — a CT2 placed structure built from a kit (Plank ×6, Cloth ×4, Rope ×6, plus a Copper Pantry — **requires Advanced Copper Tools**) that can only be set up inside the Village. Drop trade goods into its inventory; one item sells automatically per day, building a Sales Revenue balance you collect as copper nuggets (50 revenue each) or salt (30 revenue each). Unattended stalls have a small chance of being robbed every 7 days
- **Village Well** — a buildable CT2 structure in the Village. The Inn Keeper hands over his **Well Plans** during his week-3 backstory; keep them on hand while at the Village and the **Village Well** blueprint appears in the crafting journal (same construction tab as the cottages). Once built, the well stands on the village board like any other structure — drag an empty water container onto it to **Draw Water** (300 units, free, any time). The dig itself consumes the plans, and once the well is finished the plans card offers a one-time choice while you stand at it: **Discard the Old Sketch** (keeps the well's original stonework) or **Re-face the Well** (re-skins it with forest-cut stone instead of the original stonework). Counts toward Village Renown either way, and finishing it earns the Inn Keeper's one-time village toast
- **Miller's Cottage** — a buildable CT2 structure in the Village, raised in seven stages on the shared village construction backbone (foundation and walls — Heavy Stone/Tree Logs; frame — Tree Logs; roof — Planks; finishing — Rope ×8, Wood ×12); grants a Comfort +14 passive. Permanent civic growth — no dismantle/pack-up. A week after it's finished, **the Miller moves in** (see Cottage Residents below)
- **Weaver's Cottage** — a second Village cottage on the same seven-stage backbone, finished with cloth and cord (Cloth ×14, Fibers ×10, Rope ×12); grants a Comfort +10 passive. Also permanent, no dismantle. A week after it's finished, **the Weaver moves in** (see Cottage Residents below)
- **Village Hall** — the village's civic capstone: the same seven-stage backbone crowned with a civic finishing stage (Plaster ×20, Clay ×16, Heavy Stone ×10). Its Notice Board (a visible progress bar) reports the hidden **Village Renown** score, tallied from Miller's Cottage, Weaver's Cottage, the Village Well, the River Bridge, and a Market Stall revenue milestone. Once Renown is full, Market Stall sales earn a 25% bonus
- **Village Hall — the Boards room** — enter the Hall to find seven notice boards. Five belong to the villagers (Inn Keeper, Miller, Weaver, Apothecary, Professor): each opens straight to that villager's notices, without a redundant "Read board" button, and now renders those notices in the board's main text area rather than cramming them into the action row. The current active errand and hint appear once one's been offered, alongside a non-spoiling teaser for the next likely errand before it arrives, a relationship-standing note, and a short log of the last few errands you've completed for them (the five cross-villager side quests get a matching teaser on both participants' boards). The **Village Standing** board reports the village's Renown standing in prose and the finished works already counting toward it. The **Village Projects** board tracks all seven village structures (both cottages, the Apothecary's Cabin, the Hall itself, the Well, the River Bridge, and the Market Stall) as planned or complete
- **The Town Watch** — four guards keep the village, each with a distinct job. **Captain Reeve Sterling** holds the village square and no longer patrols for trouble himself — he only moves when summoned. **Guard Nella Thorne** and **Guard Old Corrin** work the village square together on the day shift (05:00–20:00) and personally attack any thief or attacker they catch there. **Guard Iris Vane** takes the night watch — ordinarily out only between 20:00 and 05:00 — but once Village Crime reaches the Enemy/Banished band she hunts you down anywhere on the map, day or night, and doesn't fight you herself: she calls the rest of the Watch in. **Talk** to Captain Sterling and, the first time your Village Crime reaches the Suspected band (1-24), he gives you one formal warning — a hidden marker means it only ever happens once per run. From the Wanted band (25-59) onward, any guard sharing your current spot reacts without fighting: their Suspicion rises, and a conversation with the Captain in that band gets a harder, visibly hostile line in place of his usual greeting — neither reaction is a fight or a chase; those still only arm at 60+. Each carries a hidden **Suspicion** rating (Iris the sharpest, Corrin the slowest to suspect) that the planned theft- and attack-detection systems will read. Each guard can be **attacked** from their inspection panel: a confirmation popup first (backing out is free), then **+35 Village Crime the instant the fight begins**, win or lose. Guards are tuned to break and flee rather than die — morale drains about twice as fast as blood — so a beaten guard normally routs alive; actually killing one takes real determination and pins Village Crime at 100 (Banished, permanently). Old Corrin gives up earliest and is the hardest to kill; Nella Thorne holds out longest and so is the most likely to die. A guard standing in the same place when the fight starts registers it immediately. **Guard pursuit, once Village Crime reaches 60+** (the Enemy/Banished band — two attacks on the Watch gets you there): Thorne and Corrin drop their square beat and pathfind toward wherever you actually are, one node per move, re-routing as you flee, but only while you're on village-side ground — cross the river and they break off and walk home (a guard may overshoot the boundary by one node first, since the check runs when they pick their next move). Iris Vane has no such limit — she pathfinds toward you anywhere on the map — and the instant she reaches you she calls Captain Sterling in rather than fighting. Drop back below 60 Crime and the Watch stands down.

  Only one fight can run at a time, so the guards **queue rather than gang up**: whoever reaches you first fights while the others keep closing, and the next one starts the instant the previous fight ends. A guard who survives a fight — routed, or left standing when you fled or lost — carries their remaining blood, stamina and morale into the next one, so a run of duels plays as one running battle rather than several fresh ones. Beating a guard (killing them *or* breaking their morale) takes them out of the Watch for one **season**; they return to their post once it passes. Put **all four** down and the village drops the charges — Village Crime resets to 0 and you are Clean again. **Lose and the Watch has you, eventually**: losing to Thorne or Corrin doesn't end the fight in the Jail — they pin you down where you fell and send for the Captain instead. Only Captain Sterling's own fight, whether he was summoned or reached you directly, ends the chase: a sword cut, a stretch of **Bruising**, and a trip to the **Village Jail**, a stone lock-up beside the Village Hall, with a sentence of roughly one day per 8 points of Village Crime (capped at 8 days). The cell door has no handle from the inside — the "Walk out" action simply is not there until your sentence runs out — and the door itself tells you how many days are left. Each day served takes a day off the sentence and pays down your crime score at the same rate, and walking out clears the record entirely. **A warden is on the jail step most hours**: the four guards split the day into four non-overlapping shift blocks (Sterling 05:00-11:00, Thorne 11:00-17:00, Corrin 17:00-23:00, Vane 23:00-05:00) and leave their beats for the jail during their own block, so there is always someone rostered. Roughly once every twelve hours the whole Watch is briefly off the step at the same time — a couple of hours where the jail is genuinely unguarded, and that's your chance: shove the cell's bed aside to reveal a **Hidden Tunnel** underneath, then **Dig** — each dig costs 30 minutes and only works while the step is empty; the button is hidden outright, not just greyed out, the instant a guard is back on post. Get caught with the tunnel still exposed when the Watch returns and it caves back in — progress resets, the bed slides back over it on its own, and the sentence grows by half its original length plus a small crime bump for the attempt. Move the bed back in time and there's no penalty; the dig is simply picked back up next window. Dig enough and **Crawl through the tunnel** appears: it clears whatever's left of the sentence, drops you outside the walls on the Village Path, and brands you an escaped convict — worse to be caught again than a first-time arrest. **A meager ration keeps you alive while you serve**: a bowl of gruel and a jug of water appear in the cell once each day, enough to hold your Weight and Hydration steady without letting you thrive, and a disableable emergency top-up (`EnableJailSafetyNet` config, on by default) catches either one if it ever drops dangerously low regardless of cause. Sitting still is optional: **Meditate** passes a couple of hours doing nothing in particular, the cell's own bed doubles as a normal Nap/Sleep spot, and counting the stones in the floor pays off if you keep at it. The Jail and its cell currently ship with blank placeholder art, and only guards are attackable — the named villagers are not. Requires **CSFFModFramework 2.20.0+** (`NPCCharacterPerk` JSON loading); on an older framework the guards spawn without their Suspicion ratings
- **Village timeline — the Inn Keeper's introduction** — the village opens in phases, at your pace. Until you present yourself to the Inn Keeper and finish his introduction conversation (how money works in the valley, and the construction projects the village dreams of), the Professor only redirects you to the Inn, neither NPC trades, Academy study is politely refused, and the Miller's Cottage / Weaver's Cottage / Village Hall / Village Well blueprints stay out of the crafting journal. Completing the intro opens all of it at once and starts a hidden **Village Clock**: the Inn Keeper's follow-up conversations pace from *your* introduction date — his settled-in line at village week 2, his backstory at week 3, the odd-jobs errand from week 2, the seasonal fireside stories from week 4, and Ash's disappearance no earlier than week 6 — never from the world calendar, and never more than one onboarding beat per conversation. Ask him to "remind me how the village works" any time for a recap. Existing saves with real village progress (the Well Plans, or any completed villager quest) are detected automatically and skip the intro
- **Village Inn** — stands in the Village from day one; enter it like a vanilla cabin. Drop Salt, metal Nuggets, or (if the optional DurosCoinage mod is installed) a Duros Coinage Metal Coin onto the Inn Counter to fund an Inn Account (holds up to 500); **Purchase Meal** and **Stay the Night** each draw 50 from that balance. **Work Odd Jobs** — a 4-hour shift paying 1-2 copper nuggets at 50% quality — needs no balance at all
- **Inn Keeper** — a resident NPC inside the Village Inn. **Chat** (1 hour) boosts Loneliness, Wellbeing, Isolation, Comfort, Morale, Socials, and Stress. **Trade** buys and sells food, spices, and salt from the inn pantry (sells at 1.5× value, pays 0.5×; the pantry restocks a few staples every morning). **Talk** opens a fireside dialog: hear a tale of the forest's real places and legends (the River Clearing, the Old Woods, the Wandering Oak, the Hag of the Black Mire, the Primeval Wolf...), and the conversation turns to **Ash, the inn's lost cat** — agree to help and Ash starts prowling the **Foraging Forest between dusk and dawn (17:00–06:00)**. Win his trust with meat or fish and he becomes a keepable pet (Feed / Give Water / Pet / Play, a passive Loneliness aura, and hunger/thirst to mind — starve him and he wanders off). If a **Rain Cistern with water** sits on the same board he'll drink from it on his own whenever his Thirst runs low, but every self-serve drink chips away at his visible **Care** (relationship) stat — Pet, Feed, or personally Give Water to keep it up; leave him entirely to the cistern and his Care runs dry after about nine in-game days and he wanders off. Report back to the Inn Keeper for a small reward. Around village week 6, Ash may pick up the scent of a wild boar and go tracking it toward **Hunter's Crossing** — the Inn Keeper's check-in dialog will hint at where he's gone; follow his trail and confront the boar to send Ash home safely with a reward and a boar carcass. **Chatting builds a hidden Friendship level**: at 15+ it unlocks a warmer greeting variant, a one-time **Inn Regular** perk (slows Loneliness/Stress buildup), and steadily better Trade prices (markup falls from 1.5× toward 1.2×, buyback rises from 0.5× toward 0.65× as Friendship approaches 20). Drag foraged **Wild Garlic** onto him as a one-time gift to instantly unlock the **Market Stall** blueprint. From village week 2 (counted from his introduction) he'll occasionally ask for a hand — deliver **Dried Wild Garlic** for a small reward, once per season. His pantry stock now varies by season (fresh Billberries in summer, Roasted Acorns in autumn, Fermented Billberries and Dried Bird Meat in winter, and more). He'll remark once each on the **Village Well**, **River Bridge**, **Miller's Cottage**, **Weaver's Cottage**, and **Village Hall** the first time you talk to him after each is built, and again once Village Renown crosses its halfway and full marks; once the Well is finished he invites you to a one-time village toast. Ask him about the Professor for a bit of cross-NPC banter
- **Village Academy** — stands in the Village from day one; enter it like a vanilla cabin (study opens once you've heard the Inn Keeper's introduction). Drop Salt, metal Nuggets, or a Duros Coinage Metal Coin (see Requirements) onto the Lecture Hall lectern to fund a Tuition Account (holds up to 1,000). Six courses run as 2-hour study sessions — Architecture (72 h), Metallurgy (60 h), Herbalism (30 h), and Fishing/Armorer/Medicine (24 h each) — with a one-time 100-currency enrollment fee drawn from the Tuition Account on each course's first session. Passing a course's Final Exam grants a hidden Graduate perk: Architecture unlocks the Water-Driven Sawmill and Grinding Mill (plus, together with Metallurgy, the Water-Driven Forge and Workshop Kit); Metallurgy unlocks the Copper Sheet recipe; Herbalism doubles forage yields; Fishing unlocks the Iron Fishing Rod recipe and improves the Iron Rod's catch odds; Armorer unlocks the copper and iron armor recipes (requires Advanced Copper Tools — the course is hidden from the lectern if ACT isn't installed); Medicine speeds wound clotting and pain recovery. All of these blueprints stay locked for every character until the matching course is passed
- **Professor** — a resident NPC who lodges at the Inn and forages the village outskirts by day until his satchel fills, then takes up his post at the Academy. Until you've heard the Inn Keeper's introduction he stays at the Academy and politely redirects you to the Inn — no trade, no wandering. After it, **Talk** opens a greeting dialog that varies by season and by how well he knows you (with a branch that only appears after day 3). **Trade** buys his foraged specimens — course graduates unlock rarer specialty stock — with markup/buyback improving as you earn his trust. Take his **weekly specimen errand** from the commissions board (a repeatable Nettle Leaves request), his **Field Samples** task once you've settled in a while (day 10+, five Billberries), and — once you've graduated Herbalism — his **Applied Herbalism** task (four Dried Nettle Leaves); ask "Any work for me?" once one's on offer. Try the **entrance-exam quiz** for a small prize. He hands out **milestone gifts** for your first degree, for graduating all six courses, and (at Trust 80+) a stone-tiles gift for a scholar's long-earned confidence; he remembers where he foraged that day, remarks once on village news (a finished river bridge, the Apothecary's Cabin, the Village Hall, or the innkeeper's cat taking to you), and reacts once to each of the six Academy courses the first time you pass it
- **Cottage Residents — the Miller & the Weaver** — each cottage gains a resident NPC **one week (7 in-game days) after you finish building it**; they appear at the Village the next time you visit on or after their move-in day. Both offer **Talk** (a short dialog about their craft and their new home) and **Chat** (1 hour; the same Loneliness/Stress/Comfort social boost as chatting with the Inn Keeper). Neither trades — they're neighbors, not shopkeepers. The move-in countdown is stored in a hidden per-resident stat, so it survives saving and reloading. Both cottages are now **enterable** ("Enter the Cottage"/"Exit"), and each resident keeps a daily schedule: home inside their own cottage overnight (22:00–6:00), standing at the Village the rest of the day, with a guaranteed evening visit to the Inn and one fixed day a week at the Academy (staggered so Miller and Weaver never go the same day), plus the occasional half-day spent wandering the outdoor map instead. A resident never wanders off mid-conversation, and won't head to the Inn/Academy until you've visited that interior yourself at least once. Like the Professor and the Apothecary, a resident now walks each commute tile by tile rather than jumping straight to the destination
- **The Miller's Copper Chest** — a banded storage chest stands inside the Miller's cottage interior. Once he has moved in, he adds a little **Salt** and a little **Wheat Flour** to it roughly once a week, the first time you step inside on or after the due day — up to a ceiling of 300 in salt value and 10 goods cards, after which that half of the accrual pauses. This **replaces** the Miller's old weekly satchel restock rather than adding to it; his own carried trade stock is now just what he started with. The chest drives two things. **Sell to the Miller**: drag a valuable item onto the chest and he buys it at the same weight-based price the Market Stall uses — but he pays out of the chest's actual contents, so the sale is refused outright (nothing consumed) if he can't currently afford it. A newly settled Miller can't meet the price of anything expensive; the same Miller several weeks later can. Payment comes out of his own hoard, smallest coin first, and he'll overpay slightly rather than block a sale he can't make exact change for. **Search for valuables**: take everything in the chest for yourself, at a risk. If the Miller is home you are caught outright; otherwise it's a roll — 15% base, rising 5% per earlier undetected theft this season (that heat resets each season, and whenever you're finally caught), plus 20% while Iris Vane's night watch is out. Getting caught adds 10 to the hidden Village Crime score; getting away with it leaves no trace at all. Emptying the chest also empties the Miller's purse, so a robbed Miller can't buy anything until he has saved up again. **Miller only for now** — the Weaver, Apothecary, Inn Keeper, and Professor have no chest yet
- **Miller's Cottage Grinding / Weaver's Cottage Crafting** — each cottage's own crafting blueprints (grinding wheat/rye/acorn flour for the Miller; rope, large cloth, and fiber processing for the Weaver) remain available at the cottage exterior at any time, independent of the resident's schedule
- **Apothecary's Cabin** — a buildable structure in the Foraging Forest, unlocked only by graduating the Academy's Herbalism course. Its finishing stage bundles the valley's rarest dried herbs — Dried Hemp Flower, Dried Ginseng, Dried Reishi, and Dried Nettle Leaves — together with a tamed Shadow (see below), who is consumed as part of the finishing ritual. **Requires the Herbs & Fungi mod** — three of those four herbs are H&F items with no Community Mod Chest substitute, so the cabin cannot be finished without it installed
- **Apothecary — daily schedule** — once you've gifted her a Market Stall Kit she commutes daily: home in her cabin overnight, staffing the Market Stall through the day, a nightly visit to the Inn (19:15–21:00, matching Miller/Weaver's guaranteed Inn evenings), and one Academy afternoon (13:00–17:00) every week — each leg with its own matching card art. **Trade** restocks her herb/tincture shelf every few days. Drop a rare **Herb Mixture** on her and she pauses her whole schedule for two days to brew a Powerful Healing Potion
- **Shadow the Cat** — a second, independent companion cat, unrelated to Ash. Once you've graduated Herbalism, he starts turning up in the **Foraging Forest between dusk and dawn (17:00–06:00)** — pitch-black and easy to walk right past until he moves. He isn't won over with food: play a **Wooden Flute, Bone Flute, or Frame Drum** near him and he stops to listen (the instrument isn't consumed). Once tamed he's fed and watered like any companion (Feed / Give Water / Pet, hunger/thirst to mind — and the same visible **Care** stat as Ash: he'll self-serve from a nearby **Rain Cistern** when thirsty, but each drink lowers his Care unless you tend him yourself), and he's the final requirement for finishing the **Apothecary's Cabin** above — consumed in the cabin's final construction stage

## Requirements

- BepInEx 5.x
- **CSFFModFramework 2.15.1+** — JSON loading and injection, the WorldMap location injection used by the Village area, the framework APIs used by the armor combat patch (`Patcher/ArmorCardsPatch.cs`), `CharacterPerkPerkGroup: "None"` (framework 2.11.0+) used by the six Academy graduate perks so they stay out of character creation and are only granted at runtime on course completion, and non-UID ScriptableObject registration **plus body `*WarpData` resolution (framework 2.15.1+)** that the Inn Keeper / Professor / Miller / Weaver `DialogScene`/`DialogLine` assets load through. On an older framework the dialogs silently never resolve (the Talk button opens nothing) and the graduate perks leak into character creation for free. The vanilla trading-value table additionally needs **framework 2.18.0+** (`TradingValues.json` loader); below that it is skipped harmlessly. The winter Village road blockage additionally needs **framework 2.19.0+** (`SealableGates` `"Season"`-trigger support); below that the snow drifts never appear and the roads silently never seal for winter. The hidden Village Crime tracker rises from attacking a guard (+35), killing one (+100, an instant Banished), getting caught robbing the Miller's Copper Chest (+10), or getting caught mid-dig in the jail escape tunnel, on any framework version. Its banishment travel lock additionally needs **framework 2.20.2+** (`ConnectionGates` `LockConditions` parsing — 2.20.0/2.20.1 declare the field but never read it from JSON); below that the lock is ignored and the Village Path stays governed by the bridge/pathfinder gate alone, even though Crime itself is still tracked and the guards still pursue.
- **Advanced Copper Tools** — hard dependency for the River Bridge (unlocks the Village area), the Market Stall blueprint (consumes a Copper Pantry), and the Copper Bed Frame blueprint (consumes a Copper Sheet, one of the two components of the Comfortable Bed); also required for the Academy's Armorer course to have a payoff — that course's Study/Final Exam actions are hidden entirely if ACT isn't installed.
- **DurosCoinage** (optional) — enables dropping a Duros Coinage Metal Coin as a third currency option at the Inn Counter and Academy lectern. Not required: Salt and metal Nuggets fund both accounts on their own.
- **Water-Driven Infrastructure** (optional) — the Academy's Architecture and Metallurgy courses unlock the Water-Driven Sawmill, Grinding Mill, Forge, and Workshop; those specific blueprints only exist if WDI is installed. The Professor's Metallurgy-graduate specialty trade stock also occasionally offers a couple of WDI components (copper gear, copper rivet) alongside his usual finds — those two rolls simply never appear without WDI installed. Every other course payoff (Copper Sheet, Iron Fishing Rod, copper/iron armor recipes, and the Herbalism/Medicine perks) is independent of WDI.
- **Herbs & Fungi** — hard dependency for finishing the **Apothecary's Cabin**. Its finishing construction stage requires Dried Hemp Flower, Dried Ginseng, and Dried Reishi (all H&F items, reused directly from the existing Healing Mixture recipe) alongside a vanilla herb and a tamed Shadow the Cat. Without H&F installed, the Apothecary's Cabin can be started but never finished. Every other village structure (both cottages, the Village Hall) is independent of H&F.
- **Sirus23 Mod Collection** (optional) — the Professor's Medicine-graduate specialty trade stock occasionally offers a Lanolin Salve alongside his usual finds; that one roll never appears without this mod installed. Nothing else in Community Mod Chest depends on it.

**Dependency chain:** `CSFFModFramework` → `AdvancedCopperTools` → `Community Mod Chest`. CMC declares ACT, H&F, Water-Driven Infrastructure, and Sirus23 Mod Collection all as `SoftDependency` (for BepInEx load order — the mod still loads without any of them present), but River Bridge, Market Stall, the Copper Bed Frame/Comfortable Bed, and the Academy Armorer course are non-functional without ACT actually installed, and the Apothecary's Cabin cannot be completed without H&F actually installed. WDI and Sirus23 Mod Collection are both fully optional — their absence only removes a couple of specialty trade-stock rolls from the Professor.

> **Note:** The Wisp's Cabin location and the Wisp deed/quest tracking system are **benched** pending the upcoming vanilla NPC rework. The full design + working code is archived under `Documentation/Ideas/Community_Mod_Chest/Wisp_and_NPCs/`.

## Install

Copy the mod folder into `BepInEx/plugins/Community_Mod_Chest/` with `Community_Mod_Chest.dll` inside it.

On a clean load, the log shows: `Community Mod Chest v1.45.0 loaded.`
